# Known problems in geometry kernels, and where they come from

**Decision question: which failure modes of existing kernels are consequences of their design,
and how does our design avoid each one — or knowingly accept it?**

**Status:** seeded 2026-09-09 from phase 1 findings and general knowledge — **every row is a
hypothesis until a candidate note supplies evidence.** Rows without evidence are marked as such.
**Serves:** ADR-0004, ADR-0005, ADR-0006; kernel spec §4–§6.

---

## How to read this table

One row per problem. A problem is something that is observed to go wrong for users — a boolean
that produces a broken shell, a reference that breaks after an edit, a model that stops being
interactive. The **root cause** column names the design choice the problem traces back to, not
the symptom. The **evidence** column points at the candidate note section or issue that shows
it; until it does, the row's status is `hypothesis`.

The last column is the reason this file exists: what the kernel spec does about it, with the
spec section, so the design decision is traceable back to the problem it answers.

| # | Problem | Root cause (hypothesised or shown) | Seen in | Evidence | Status | Our design's answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-01 | Boolean produces an invalid shell, a wrong result, or fails outright on coplanar / touching input | Floating-point predicates with tolerance-based topology decisions; the topology is decided on rounded values, so it can contradict itself. **Blender narrows the cause:** exact predicates remove the coplanar half of this, and what survives is the other half — the *contract*, not the arithmetic (which shapes must appear in the output, what happens when the input is not closed, whether a refusal is distinguishable from a result) | OCCT; **Blender — all three of its booleans, in different ways** (Carve until 2018, the float BMesh solver still shipping as *Fast*, and the exact solver) | [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Known problems, §Measurements; [`blender.md`](blender.md) §Known problems (20 tracker issues, four failures found ourselves), §Measurements, §Carve history. Measured: Blender's *Fast* solver is wrong on 3 of 10 torture cases and non-deterministic on one; its *Exact* solver returned a valid, closed, manifold union of **one** cube for two cubes touching at a point, with no warning; the maintainer who removed Carve recorded that Carve and the BMesh solver shared "the same level of limitations about manifold meshes and touching edges" | shown | Exact predicates decide topology; constructed points are rounded but never decide (spec §5). **Added from Blender:** a degraded path returns a *different result value* rather than the same one (spec §12) — Blender's exact solver silently becomes a float ray-cast heuristic when the input is not closed — and a refusal never returns the unmodified first operand |
| P-02 | Boolean succeeds but the result is slightly wrong (slivers, gaps, misplaced vertices) | Tolerance growth: each operation may widen entity tolerances, and downstream operations inherit the looser value | OCCT tolerant B-rep | [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Tolerance model (measured: a fuse raised its *inputs*' tolerance 1.5e-7 → 5.0e-4; glued gaps added 3.3e-6 m³ per operation, linearly); **[`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §Known problems, §Measurements — the same shape in a mesh kernel**: a boolean's result tolerance is `max(inP.tolerance_, inQ.tolerance_)` and is never re-derived from the *output* bounding box ([`boolean_result.cpp#L828-L836`](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/blob/0edd9d54876f3135e431575214dd6d8a72866fee/src/boolean_result.cpp#L828-L836), read at the tag; reported as [#1714](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1714), on which no maintainer commented). Measured, the other face of it: raising the tolerance to 1e-6 m deleted the benchmark's 1e-7 m slab entirely and reported `NoError` | shown | Tolerances are explicit, per operation and per entity, grow but never shrink, and are reported (spec §5). **Added from Manifold:** a result's tolerance is derived from the result, never inherited as a maximum over the operands — and a simplification that removes a shell is a reported fact with a changed result value, never a `NoError` (spec §12) |
| P-03 | Exact boolean is correct but too slow or memory-hungry for interactive use | Exact rational arithmetic on every construction, not only on predicates | CGAL Nef polyhedra | Nothing yet for CGAL. **A third point on the trade exists:** [`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §How the boolean works — Manifold uses neither exact constructions nor exact predicates. Its predicates are double-precision with a symbolic tie-break that fires only when two coordinates compare *bit-identically* ([`shared.h#L90-L96`](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/blob/0edd9d54876f3135e431575214dd6d8a72866fee/src/shared.h#L90-L96)), and it was numerically correct on 9 of our 10 cases while running 19× faster than Blender's exact-rational solver on the twelve-opening wall. Its own maintainers name the limit: on [#676](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/676) `elalish` (OWNER) closes a precision report "working as intended" because the coordinates were no longer bit-identical | hypothesis for CGAL; the exactness/speed trade is **shown to have more than two positions** | Exact arithmetic only in predicates, adaptive with a floating filter (spec §5). **Warning for ADR-0005, from Manifold:** our torture set is built from exact, bit-identical coordinates, which is exactly the input class a symbolic tie-break wins. The set cannot distinguish exact predicates from that tie-break, and no ADR may cite the 9/10 as evidence that it can |
| P-04 | References break after an edit (dimensions, openings, features point at the wrong or a vanished face) | Topological naming: references store names for the *output* of a computation, and a kernel with no cross-operation history (OCCT: `BRepTools_History` covers one operation only) forces the application to reconstruct identity from scratch after every recompute | FreeCAD on OCCT; all history-based CAD | [`freecad-topological-naming.md`](freecad-topological-naming.md) §The mechanism, §When the reference breaks, §Evidence, §Measurements — mechanism traced to `src/App/ElementMap.cpp`/`TopoShapeExpansion.cpp` at FreeCAD tag `1.0.0`; five dated GitHub issues (2024-07 to 2026-04, three post-1.0); one measured case (topology-preserving edit survives, byte-identical `MappedName`) | shown | Out of kernel scope: references point at semantic entities in `app/` (R-13). **Corrected from earlier phrasing:** generational handles (spec §6) are a memory-safety mechanism (stable *within one build*, detect dangling access) — they do not by themselves give recompute-across-rebuilds identity, which is a different problem FreeCAD's case study shows costs years of dedicated engineering to even partially solve. For references outside the semantic-entity boundary, `app/` needs its own history-reconstruction layer, built on per-operation generated/modified reporting from the kernel, with breakage surfaced visibly (never silently repaired) and an explicit, bounded trace depth (see `freecad-topological-naming.md` §What this means for our design) |
| P-05 | Precision loss far from the origin; geometry vibrates or snaps | Single global origin, float32 storage or GPU float32 with world coordinates | Real-time engines; float32 viewers; **Blender** (`Mesh` positions `float3`, `BMesh` `float co[3]`, object transforms `float4x4`) | phase 1 spatial-model note; **[`blender.md`](blender.md) §Precision — measured**: float32 ULP at 500 km is 0.03125 m; the benchmark wall lost **0.15 m³** of volume and **0.0348 m** of vertex position at 500 km on the exact solver, against OCCT's 2.65e-10 m on the same test — a factor of ~10⁸ explained by one type declaration. Also measured at *unit* scale: the 1e-7 m `sliver` operand arrives 19% too thick because `0.5 + 1e-7` is not representable; **[`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §Precision — measured, and it separates the two causes**: the identical algorithm on `Mesh64` (float64) input held the 500 km case to **3.3e-11 m** and −1.1e-11 relative volume, while `Mesh` (float32) input gave **0.0186 m** and the same +0.15 m³ Blender reported — so the loss is the storage type, not the algorithm. A **second, independent** mechanism was found there: Manifold derives its epsilon as `kPrecision * Box::Scale()`, and `Box::Scale()` is the largest **absolute coordinate**, so one unit box gets a tolerance of 1e-12 m at the origin and 5.00e-07 m at 500 km on the f64 path — 0.0596 m on the f32 path. Distance from a projection origin sets a tolerance even where the precision is adequate | **shown (measured)** | f64 in tile-local frames, camera-relative f32 only at upload (spec §5, ADR-0006). **Sharpened by the measurement:** moving the 500 km into the object transform restored the result to **0 m** deviation, so local frames do work — but Blender's frame origin is itself float32, so *the frame origin must be f64 even when the geometry inside it is f32*. That is the half ADR-0006 must be explicit about. **Confirmed independently on Manifold:** carrying the offset in `Manifold.translate()` (applied in double) left the float32 result byte-identical to the origin run, **0 m** — and raised the tolerance from 1.5e-12 to 5e-07 all the same, so a local frame buys back the geometry and **not** the tolerance. Our tolerances are per-entity and must never be a function of where the project sits in a national grid |
| P-06 | Whole model re-evaluates after one change | Batch conversion architecture; no dependency tracking below the model level | Most IFC tooling; viewers | — | hypothesis (R-01) | Kernel operations are per-object and handle-addressed; the evaluation graph lives in `app/` (spec §3) |
| P-07 | Interactivity collapses around 10⁴–10⁵ objects | Per-object datablock cost in the *evaluation graph*, before any drawing — not (only) per-object draw calls. The graph's unit is the datablock, and one model element must be one datablock to stay selectable and attributable | **Blender itself** — measured with no add-on in the process, which resolves R-03's "Blender or Bonsai" in one direction | phase 1 hosts note; **[`blender.md`](blender.md) §Incremental evaluation, §Measurements — measured** in [`spikes/blender-ceiling/`](../../../spikes/blender-ceiling/): one `view_layer.update()` after moving **one** object costs 1.14 ms at 1k objects, **16.3 ms at 10k**, **332.1 ms at 100k**; the identical triangles as a single multi-shell object cost 0.005 / 0.013 / 0.018 ms. Memory: 81 MB / 619 MB / **6.2 GB** for the 1k / 10k / 100k object scenes, i.e. ~63–65 kB per additional object. Headless, `--factory-startup`, no viewport | **partly shown** — shown for the evaluation graph and shown to be Blender's rather than Bonsai's; the viewport half of R-03 is still unmeasured, and a headless driver cannot measure it | Structure-of-arrays, generational handles, instancing-first GPU layouts (spec §6). **Added from the measurement:** the design question is not "how fast is one graph node" but "how many nodes does one edit touch" — an evaluation graph with one node per building element hits this wall in any language |
| P-08 | Single-threaded evaluation | Global state, non-reentrant kernels, locks around shared caches | OCCT (partially), IFC parsers | **Not shown for OCCT** — [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Measurements: parallelism is opt-in and off by default but it works (9.5× measured on curved tessellation); no thread-safety defect found on the tracker. The measured OCCT problem is the opposite one: the same flag costs +8% on average (+1.3% to +13.5% over four paired deflections) when per-item work is too small. Restate the row, or carry it on the IFC parsers alone | hypothesis | No global state; `parallel for` over independent handles; DynLex threading added when needed (spec §6, §8) |
| P-09 | Mesh boolean requires manifold, self-intersection-free input and rejects real models | The algorithm's correctness proof assumes it, and the ingest checks the assumption before anything else | Manifold — the standalone library **and** as shipped inside Blender since 4.5 | [`blender.md`](blender.md) §The other two solvers, §Known problems, **and [`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §The contract, §Known problems, §Measurements — the same library measured directly at v3.5.3**. Measured through Blender (which bundles v3.2.1 and feeds it float32): the only solver to get all ten torture cases right, 9–12× faster than the exact solver on the twelve-opening wall, and it refused the benchmark's T-junction operand ("Cannot execute, non-manifold inputs") and returned the unmodified first operand. Measured standalone: the same operand is refused on **both** the f32 and the f64 input path, with `Error::NotManifold` and an **empty** result that the boolean then propagates ([`spikes/boolean-torture/results/manifold.md`](../../../spikes/boolean-torture/results/manifold.md) Probe C); the `Merge` helper the README offers for "slightly non-manifold meshes" does not repair a T-junction. Tracker: [#139370](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/139370), [#140590](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/140590), [#144698](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/144698) (Blender, first two answered by the boolean's own author) and upstream [#1348](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1348), [#1354](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1354), [#1364](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1364) | shown | Mesh validity check is explicit; healing is an explicit operation; arrangement-based boolean tolerates non-manifold input where the literature allows (spec §4, §12). **Added from Blender:** a refusal must be a distinguishable return value, not a message plus the first operand — and [`manifold.md`](manifold.md) shows the library itself gets this right while its most prominent embedder discards it at the boundary, so that defect belongs to the binding. **Sharpened by the standalone measurement:** the refusal is real, but the *field's* dominant complaint about this library is not refusal — it is ε-valid-but-ugly accepted output (slivers, degenerate triangles), which its correctness contract permits by design. A kernel must therefore state what its output guarantees geometrically, not only topologically |

| P-11 | Tessellation quality vs. speed: chords too coarse for drawings, too fine for the viewport | One tessellation per shape, one parameter set | OCCT, IFC viewers | [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Measurements — the *architectural* half holds (OCCT caches one `Poly_Triangulation` per face; measured, a finer request replaces it at full cost and a coarser request is silently ignored); the *cost* half does not on planar geometry (four decades of deflection changed the time 14% and the triangle count not at all). It is a curved-geometry problem | partly shown | Tessellation is a conversion with explicit deflection parameters; multiple results per object are allowed (spec §4) |
| P-12 | Curved-surface booleans fail where planar ones succeed | Surface–surface intersection is numerically hard; marching produces gaps | OCCT | [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Known problems — seven OCCT issues and two FreeCAD issues with `curved-intersection` as the trigger, against zero curved failures we measured ourselves (the torture set is entirely planar, so it cannot show this) | partly shown | Planar B-rep booleans first (M5), curved in M8 after measurement; mesh path as the fallback the caller chooses (spec §9) |
| P-13 | Point clouds exceed memory | In-core storage | PCL (in-core by default) | — | hypothesis | Chunked, memory-mapped storage with an octree from the first milestone that holds points (spec §4, §8) |
| P-14 | Licence blocks embedding or later open-sourcing | Copyleft on the kernel | some candidates | — | hypothesis | Not a concern for an own kernel; a concern for anything borrowed as code rather than as design (R-11) |
| P-15 | A single modelling operation consumes unbounded memory or runs unbounded time, and the caller cannot cancel it or find out how far it got | The operation owns the whole computation: intermediate structures grow with the intersection graph and have no ceiling, and the inner loops poll no cancellation flag, so a deadline outside the call cannot be enforced from inside it | OCCT | [`occt.md`](occt.md) §Known problems — [#724](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/issues/724) and [#800](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/issues/800) report >10 GB on a *single* boolean, the second with a maintainer confirming the defect and no fast solution; [#1385](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/issues/1385) reports 619 s of CPU against a 30 s deadline that could not fire, the reporter finding no `UserBreak()` polling inside `IntTools_FaceFace::Perform`. All three are reporter diagnoses. **A counter-example exists:** [`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §Measurements — Manifold ships `ExecutionContext` with `Cancel()`, `Cancelled()` and `Progress()` ([`common.h#L250-L299`](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/blob/0edd9d54876f3135e431575214dd6d8a72866fee/include/manifold/common.h#L250-L299)), polled between phases and once per parallel chunk ([`parallel.h#L396-L440`](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/blob/0edd9d54876f3135e431575214dd6d8a72866fee/src/parallel.h#L396-L440)). Measured: a cancelled boolean returns an empty result carrying `Error::Cancelled`. The API is neither exotic nor expensive — a 23.6k-line library has it and a multi-million-line one does not | partly shown | **Half answered, and the gap is recorded rather than papered over.** Work is per-object over independent generational handles (spec §3, §6), and operations return a result value with a message instead of aborting on user input (spec §12), so a runaway operation is contained to one object. The spec does **not** yet define a cancellation token, a progress report, or a memory/time budget for a long operation; adding one is an open question against spec §6 and §12. **Manifold answers the shape of it**: a permanent cancel flag on a context object, an operation that returns a *typed cancelled result* instead of throwing, and cancellation polled at phase boundaries and inside parallel chunks — borrowable as a design and, under Apache-2.0, as code |
| P-16 | The same operation on the same input returns different geometry on different runs | Parallel evaluation without a canonical ordering at the points where partial results are merged, so the output depends on thread scheduling | Blender — measured in its float solver; reported and fixed upstream for its Manifold solver | [`blender.md`](blender.md) §Known problems — measured, Probe E in [`spikes/boolean-torture/results/blender.md`](../../../spikes/boolean-torture/results/blender.md): ten runs of one union with operands rebuilt each time produced **two distinct topologies** (32 verts/15 faces six times, 32/14 four times) at equal volume. Blender's tracker carries the same class in a different solver, [#143644](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/143644), where the **reporter** — not a maintainer — states "this is indeed a threading issue… When running Blender single threaded, this doesn't happen"; the exact solver's author then reproduced the non-determinism in the standalone Manifold library, reported it upstream and confirmed the fixed library version ships in 5.0. **[`manifold.md`](manifold.md) §Known problems adds the upstream half:** that report is one of several fixed case by case ([#1233](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1233), cross-architecture; [#1320](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1320), coplanar unions), and a global deterministic mode was **removed and deliberately not restored** — on [#1177](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/issues/1177) `pca006132` (COLLABORATOR) states that with parallelism "we do have things that are racy and are not deterministic", and `elalish` (OWNER) that "I don't think we're bringing it back as an option". Measured at v3.5.3 with TBB enabled: 10 runs of each of four case/operation pairs produced **one signature each** — the current state, not a guarantee | shown | **Determinism is an invariant, not an aspiration.** Parallel work runs over independent handles and partial results are merged in a canonical order (spec §6); a determinism test — same input, N runs, one signature — belongs in the kernel's own suite from the first boolean. Without it a result cannot be diffed, cached or regression-tested, and two people opening one file can get different geometry |

## Problems we may be about to create

Honesty in the other direction. Building our own kernel does not remove problems; it chooses
which ones we own.

| # | Problem | Why we would have it | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N-01 | Our booleans fail on inputs the mature kernels handle, because they have twenty years of fixes we do not | Every kernel's boolean is a long tail of special cases | The boolean torture set in `reference/`; exact arrangement instead of special cases; measure against OCCT and Blender on the same cases | open |
| N-02 | Compiler defects on the kernel's critical path | The kernel is the largest DynLex program | Defects are fixed in dynlex, never worked around; pinned commit; the smoke test runs on every build | open |
| N-03 | Four representations, four sets of bugs | Breadth | Each representation earns its place by a milestone with an exit criterion; a representation nobody needs is deleted | open |
| N-04 | Performance targets missed because DynLex lacks threading or SIMD codegen | Language maturity | Measured at M1 and M7; targets are recorded with the measured number, never quietly relaxed | open |
